The most accurate model was looking at the wrong thing.

01 · Findings

Accuracy alone picks the wrong model

On the held-out test set ResNet18 led on every headline metric. Looking only at the table, it's the model you would ship.

Grad-CAM tells a different story. In Figs. 1 and 2, ResNet18's heatmaps spread over the neck, mediastinum and image edges, while MobileNetV2's sit on lung tissue, where a radiologist looks. Section 02 measures this over the whole test set.

All three over-call pneumonia at the default 0.5 threshold. Sensitivity is near perfect (389 of 390 cases for ResNet18), but a third of healthy children get flagged. Choosing the threshold for 95% sensitivity instead (0.946 for ResNet18, picked by cross-validation) raises specificity from 0.68 to 0.88. The live demo uses that threshold.

02 · Attention, measured

Only one model reliably looks at the lungs

For every test image, a pretrained chest X-ray segmentation model (torchxrayvision's ChestX-Det PSPNet) outlines the lungs, clavicles and scapulae. We then measure what share of each model's Grad-CAM attention falls inside the lungs. Attention spread at random would score the lungs' share of the image, shown as the dashed line.

MobileNetV2 puts 71% of its attention on the lungs of normal children, nearly double chance. ResNet18 manages 44%, and EfficientNet-B0 41%. The gap repeats on adult NIH X-rays (58% vs 39%), where the segmenter is most reliable.

One correction to how this project started. By eye, ResNet18 seemed to lock onto the shoulders. Measured, its attention on clavicles and scapulae (4.7%) is below their 6.4% share of the image. Its attention isn't on the shoulders; it's simply not on the lungs.

03 · External test

Every model breaks on another hospital's X-rays

We scored all three models on 656 adult chest X-rays from the NIH Clinical Center, labelled by radiologists (Majkowska et al., 2020): 364 with airspace opacity, 292 with no findings. Different country, different scanners, adults instead of children.

Every model loses about 0.2 AUC. ResNet18's lead on the pediatric set is statistically real, but it disappears here: MobileNetV2 ranks first (+0.015 AUC), though the 95% CI (−0.020 to +0.051) includes zero. The pediatric threshold doesn't transfer either. At 0.946, ResNet18 flags 80% of healthy adults.

The honest reading: lung-focused attention didn't buy measurable robustness here, because the population shift swamps everything. MobileNetV2 is still the only model whose evidence a clinician could check.

05 · Method

How it was built

  1. Data

    Kermany et al. (2018) pediatric chest X-rays, 5,856 images labelled NORMAL or PNEUMONIA, ages 1–5, from Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center.

  2. Transfer learning

    ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18, MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0, each fine-tuned with a 2-class head on identical splits.

  3. Explainability

    Grad-CAM on the last convolutional block, scored against lung and shoulder masks from a pretrained segmenter. Bootstrap CIs, paired across models.

  4. Serving

    ONNX Runtime + FastAPI on Vercel. Each model also outputs its class activation map, which equals Grad-CAM for these heads, so heatmaps cost no backward pass.

06 · Limitations

What this does not show

  • One training run each. No repeated seeds, so the 1–3 point accuracy gaps between models may be partly noise.
  • The external test mixes two shifts. NIH differs in hospital and in age (adults, mostly AP views), so we can't say which one causes the drop.
  • Masks, not radiologists. Lung masks come from a model trained on adults. It misses some consolidated lung on pediatric pneumonia X-rays, which is why normal X-rays are the primary comparison.
  • Not a medical device. No FDA or CE clearance. Do not use it for clinical decisions.

Research demonstration only. This is not a diagnostic tool. Consult a qualified clinician for any medical assessment.

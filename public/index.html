Research demo · Pediatric chest radiography

The most accurate model was looking at the wrong thing.

We trained three CNNs, three seeds each, to detect pneumonia in pediatric chest X-rays. ResNet18 was the most accurate, yet only 47% of its Grad-CAM attention landed on the lungs, barely above the 39% you'd expect by chance. MobileNetV2 put 67% there, in every seed.

01 · Findings

Accuracy alone picks the wrong model

Averaged over three training seeds, ResNet18 is the most accurate model (88.2%). Looking only at accuracy, it's the model you would ship.

Accuracy at a 0.5 cut-off mostly reflects how many healthy children a model flags, though. Threshold-free AUC ties all three at 0.954–0.960. Our first single run had ResNet18 at 0.978 AUC; retraining showed that was a lucky draw (0.940–0.966 across seeds).

Grad-CAM tells a different story. In Figs. 1 and 2, ResNet18's heatmaps spread over the neck, mediastinum and image edges, while MobileNetV2's sit on lung tissue, where a radiologist looks. Section 02 measures this over the whole test set.

All three over-call pneumonia at the default 0.5 threshold. Sensitivity is near perfect (above 0.98), but 30–42% of healthy children get flagged. For the served ResNet18, choosing the threshold for 95% sensitivity instead (0.946, picked by cross-validation) raises specificity from 0.68 to 0.88. The live demo uses that threshold.

02 · Attention, measured

Only one model reliably looks at the lungs

For every test image, a pretrained chest X-ray segmentation model (torchxrayvision's ChestX-Det PSPNet) outlines the lungs, clavicles and scapulae. We then measure what share of each model's Grad-CAM attention falls inside the lungs. Attention spread at random would score the lungs' share of the image, shown as the dashed line.

Across three training seeds, MobileNetV2 puts 67% of its attention on the lungs of normal children. ResNet18 manages 47% and EfficientNet-B0 47%. Every MobileNetV2 seed beats every ResNet18 seed, on children and on adult NIH X-rays (58% vs 39%).

One correction to how this project started. By eye, ResNet18 seemed to lock onto the shoulders. Measured on the served model, its attention on clavicles and scapulae (4.7%) is below their 6.4% share of the image. Its attention isn't on the shoulders; it's simply not on the lungs.

03 · External test

Every model breaks on another hospital's X-rays

We scored all three models on 656 adult chest X-rays from the NIH Clinical Center, labelled by radiologists (Majkowska et al., 2020): 364 with airspace opacity, 292 with no findings. Different country, different scanners, adults instead of children.

Every model loses 0.13–0.20 AUC. MobileNetV2 holds up best on average (0.822 vs 0.794 for ResNet18) and beats it in two of three seeds (−0.017, +0.078, +0.026). That's a trend, not proof, with three seeds. The pediatric threshold doesn't transfer either: at 0.946, the served ResNet18 flags 80% of healthy adults.

The honest reading: the model that looks at the lungs also degrades least, but the population shift hurts every model badly. More seeds or more external data would be needed to call the robustness gain real.

05 · Method

How it was built

  1. Data

    Kermany et al. (2018) pediatric chest X-rays, 5,856 images labelled NORMAL or PNEUMONIA, ages 1–5, from Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center.

  2. Transfer learning

    ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18, MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0, each fine-tuned with a 2-class head on identical splits.

  3. Explainability

    Grad-CAM on the last convolutional block, scored against lung and shoulder masks from a pretrained segmenter. Bootstrap CIs, paired across models.

  4. Serving

    ONNX Runtime + FastAPI on Vercel. Each model also outputs its class activation map, which equals Grad-CAM for these heads, so heatmaps cost no backward pass.

06 · Limitations

What this does not show

  • Three seeds per model. Enough to show the attention gap is stable and the AUC lead was luck; too few for significance tests between architectures.
  • The external test mixes two shifts. NIH differs in hospital and in age (adults, mostly AP views), so we can't say which one causes the drop.
  • Masks, not radiologists. Lung masks come from a model trained on adults. It misses some consolidated lung on pediatric pneumonia X-rays, which is why normal X-rays are the primary comparison. On a frontal X-ray the heart overlaps the left lung, and the mask counts that overlap as lung.
  • Not a medical device. No FDA or CE clearance. Do not use it for clinical decisions.

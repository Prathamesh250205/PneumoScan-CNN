The most accurate model was looking at the wrong thing.

We trained three CNNs to detect pneumonia in 5,856 pediatric chest X-rays. ResNet18 scored highest, but Grad-CAM showed it relied on the shoulders and collarbones. MobileNetV2 scored 2.2 points lower and looked at the lungs.

01 · Findings

Accuracy alone picks the wrong model

On the held-out test set ResNet18 led on every headline metric. Looking only at the table, it's the model you would ship.

Grad-CAM tells a different story. ResNet18's activations repeatedly concentrate on non-diagnostic structure: shoulder joints, clavicles, patient positioning. Those features are correlated with the label in this dataset but have nothing to do with pneumonia. That is shortcut learning, and it tends to break on a new hospital's scanners.

MobileNetV2 attends to the pulmonary fields, which is where a radiologist looks. For deployment, that 2.2-point accuracy gap is a reasonable price for a model whose evidence can be checked.

All three share one weakness. Sensitivity is near perfect (389 of 390 pneumonia cases caught by ResNet18), but specificity is only 0.60–0.68, so roughly a third of healthy children get flagged. The training data is 3:1 pneumonia, and the 16-image validation split is too small to tune a threshold on.

03 · Method

How it was built

  1. Data

    Kermany et al. (2018) pediatric chest X-rays, 5,856 images labelled NORMAL or PNEUMONIA, ages 1–5, from Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center.

  2. Transfer learning

    ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18, MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0, each fine-tuned with a 2-class head on identical splits.

  3. Explainability

    Grad-CAM on the final convolutional block of each network, reviewed side by side on the same test images.

  4. Serving

    Exported to ONNX and served with ONNX Runtime and FastAPI. Preprocessing matches training: grayscale → 3 ch → 224² → ImageNet normalisation.

04 · Limitations

What this does not show

  • One institution. All images come from a single hospital, so no external validation was done.
  • Children only. Ages 1–5. Adult radiographs are out of distribution.
  • Qualitative attention review. Grad-CAM was inspected visually and not scored against radiologist annotations.
  • Not a medical device. No FDA or CE clearance. Do not use it for clinical decisions.

Research demonstration only. This is not a diagnostic tool. Consult a qualified clinician for any medical assessment.
